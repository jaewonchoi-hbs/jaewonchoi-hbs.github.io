I am a first-year PhD student in Accounting and Management at Harvard Business
School. My interests are in managerial accounting: how firms design
compensation and incentives, what they choose to disclose, and how governance
shapes both. I work empirically, testing theories drawn from economics,
accounting, and finance.

My current project, with Jason G. Kim, studies how the SEC's 2022 rule
requiring firms to disclose their insider trading policies changed executive
pay. We find that abnormal insider-trading profits fall as firms prepare to
comply, while total compensation rises, which suggests that trading profits had
been serving as a form of implicit pay.

Before coming to Harvard I completed an M.S. in accounting at Seoul National
University, advised by Prof. Jae Yong Shin. As a research assistant at SNU's
Center for Accounting Research I worked on human-capital measures for Korean
firms, including voluntary turnover and employee satisfaction data, and helped
host the 2026 Asia-Pacific Management Accounting Research Symposium. I studied
economics at the Wharton School, and worked in mergers and acquisitions,
private equity, and asset management in Seoul before returning to research. I
work in SAS, Stata, R, and Python.
